Kpler APIs — what we hit and why

Team note for an oil desk new to tanker freight. What each series is, the crude analogy, the economic signal, and how to defend it. Companion to the instrument primer (Baltic / DFN / TDL).

Kpler is not Baltic TD3C and not ICE TDL. Those are the price. Kpler is the physical tape — ships that can arrive, cargoes that need lifting, whether the fleet is working or stuck. Pitch: we already have the futures curve; this is tightness and positioning so we can say whether paper is rich or cheap versus the list.

1. The story in one slide

A tanker rate is the price of a scarce ship-week on a route.

  • Demand: cargoes that need a ship in a loading window (decades: 1–10, 11–20, 21–30).
  • Supply: ships that can physically be there in time (empty + ETA + speed).
  • Balance: cargoes > reachable ships → WS and $/t up, FFAs bid, some crude arbs die. Reverse → rates fall.

Same logic as crude: inventories and flows are the why; Dated / Dubai / ICE are the price. Freight demand is consumed as ton-miles (volume × distance), not barrels exported.

Usual chain: arb opens → cargoes get fixed → the regional list drains → owners gain pricing power → ballast comes in from other basins → the list rebuilds → rates normalise. Kpler is how we watch that chain without waiting for tomorrow’s Baltic.

3. Fixtures — the physical “print”

v1 Fixtures

You get: reported charters — vessel, laycan, origin/dest, status (on-subs → fully fixed / failed), Kpler rateValue in $.

Question: what got done, at what number, for which window?

Signal: more fixtures into a tight decade, or rates above the last Baltic, is spot firming before (or instead of) the index. Failed/cancelled is demand that did not clear — often a warning, not strength.

Crude analogy: a window trade. Baltic is the assessed average; fixtures are the deals.

Economics: ICE TDL averages Baltic index days in $/t, not Kpler’s dollar print. Never say “Kpler rate = TD3C.” Say “third print for colour and counts; convert to $/t; compare to ICE.”

In the build: tool B (cargo demand), daily report. Clarksons emails are the same idea, broker-shaped; Kpler is queryable history.

4. Tonnage supply — who can make the window

v1 TonnageSupplySeries + TonnageSupplyVessels

You get: daily count (or dwt) of ships that can reach a zone in N days at a chosen speed. Vessel list is the names — max 6-day slice. Series has history (we pulled 2024).

Question: how many VLCCs can be in MEG / Houston / China by this decade?

Signal: count down vs a 90-day z-score → effective supply tight → spot and near FFAs should bid, all else equal. Count up → rates should ease. Speed (11 vs 13 kn) is a scenario, like shocking a crack for runs.

Crude analogy: not “global inventories” — prompt available crude in a region. A VLCC in the USG is useless for TD3C this decade.

Economics: this is optionality of arrival. FFA traders’ “ships per cargo” in MEG is this idea. World fleet size is a different (slower) series.

In the build: core of tool A (tonnage nowcast). Backtests must snapshot daily or AIS revisions leak the future.

5. Utilisation and ballast — laden vs empty

v1 FleetUtilizationSeries · BallastCapacitySeries

You get: FleetUtilizationSeries = loaded vs ballast vs maintenance ship counts. BallastCapacitySeries = capacity/dwt/count aggregated from ballast capacity port calls (SDK example metric is Capacity in tonnes) — not the same as util Ballast count.

Question: is the fleet working or hunting?

Signal: high ballast into a load region = ships looking for cargo = bearish local rates. High laden / low ballast = already committed = bullish prompt. Yard/maintenance = supply off.

Crude analogy: refinery utilisation. High runs support cracks; idle units compress them. Ballast is unemployed ship-weeks.

Economics: charterers pay to reposition empty ships. For MEG–China VLCC, ballast in the Indian Ocean / AG is the spare inventory of the route.

In the build: tool E. Pair with the matcher: matcher = can they arrive; ballast = are they empty and looking.

6. Congestion — time destroyed at discharge

v1 CongestionSeries

You get: ships (or days) waiting before berth, e.g. China VLCCs.

Question: are ships stuck, so they miss the next load window?

Signal: queues up → effective supply down on the next load even if “global fleet” looks large. Can bid MEG rates while nameplate capacity is unchanged. Floating storage is a cousin (laden, not working).

Crude analogy: a refinery outage: nameplate unchanged, available capacity down.

Economics: congestion is a tax on fleet productivity. Ton-miles can stay high while ships available for the next decade fall.

In the build: tool E. Do not treat waiting as demand.

7. Ton-miles — demand as volume × distance

v1 FreightMetricsSeries

You get: ton-miles, ton-days, average speed/distance. Keep with_freight_view=True so we do not silently drop unsubscribed cargo.

Question: are barrels moving farther, or just more barrels?

Signal: ton-miles up with volumes flat = longer-haul (WAF / USG / Brazil → China). That uses more ship-weeks per barrel → structural bid to VLCC. Speed up = fleet working harder (tightness or catching a window).

Crude analogy: product demand vs miles to the consumer. Atlantic barrels to Asia are why TD15 / TD22 exist next to TD3C.

Economics: freight demand is not “exports.” It is tonne-miles. Use this for regime (Atlantic arb open), not a one-week scalp.

In the build: tool F. Blunt until voyage-legs (per corridor) is entitled — that call is still 403.

8. Trades (and snapshot) — the cargo, not the hire

v1 Trades · TradesSnapshot

You get: cargo legs (origin, dest, grade, vessel). Snapshot = that tape as of a past date (PIT confirmed on this account).

Question: what is actually loading/discharging, not only what was reported as a fixture?

Signal: fixture without a trade = on-subs / rumour. Trade without a fixture = owner program / CoA / restricted fleet. Snapshot stops Kpler’s later destination revisions from rewriting a backtest.

Crude analogy: cargoes in a balance vs deals in the window. Flow is oil; fixtures are ship hire. You need both.

In the build: tools B and G (arb opens → fixtures). Same stack vessel_crossing already uses.

9. Fleet development — the year story (v2 key)

v2 /fleet-development/series · /summary

You get: available (in service), deliveries (newbuilds), orders, removals (scrap/loss). Summary = this period vs previous vs last year. Date limits from 2017, ~5 years forward.

Question: is the VLCC fleet growing or shrinking over months and years?

Signal: deliveries ≫ scrap → more ships in 6–24 months → bearish deferred FFAs, not necessarily this week’s TD3C.

Crude analogy: global CDU additions. Prompt freight is geography + decades; this is nameplate.

Economics: do not put fleet size in the daily spot nowcast. Use it for “why the 2027 strip is heavy.”

In the build: structural slide / back-month context. This is what the v2 API key actually buys today.

12. Phrases that survive a challenge

  1. Kpler fixture $ is not Baltic WS and not ICE $/t; we convert and compare.
  2. Tonnage supply is reachable ships, not the world fleet.
  3. Ton-miles are demand; ballast is spare supply; congestion destroys supply.
  4. Fleet development is a year story; the matcher is a week story.
  5. We snapshot. Live Kpler revises destinations; that is why TradesSnapshot exists.
  6. The v2 key is fleet development only; next-port, routing, and Kpler arb are not entitled.
