Tanker freight instruments

A newcomer note for the oil desk: physical market, Baltic prints, ICE FFAs (monthly, balmo, daily mini), units, and worked examples. TD3C (MEG→China VLCC) is the running case. Simulated numbers unless labelled as a live snapshot.

If you already trade crude, the trap is mapping ICE Brent onto the wrong freight contract. Read the four-price picture first, then the toy October.

1. The physical market in one page

Freight is the price that clears, in a load region and fixing window, ships that can arrive in time against cargoes that need lifting. Short-run supply is almost fixed. Demand is consumed as ship-days (ton-miles), not barrels. Rates are convex: quiet when the list is long, vertical when it empties.

Usual chain: arb opens → cargoes get fixed → the regional list drains → owners gain pricing power → ballast comes in from other regions → the list rebuilds → rates normalise.

ClassTypical cargoRole

Dirty = crude and fuel oil. Clean = products in coated tanks. A fixture is a concluded charter (ship, cargo, laycan, rate). Statuses you will see on lists: on subs, fully fixed, failed, hold, relet. Raw ship counts overstate supply — many names are already on subjects or held.

Voyage charter: owner pays fuel, rate in Worldscale, $/t or lumpsum. Time charter: daily hire, charterer pays fuel.

2. Four TD3C numbers (do not mix them)

TD3C is Baltic’s VLCC Ras Tanura → Ningbo (270 kt). Paper around it is listed on ICE as wet-freight futures, cash-settled in US dollars per metric tonne.

NameICE codeWhat it isStrip you see
Baltic print(not ICE)That day’s official assessment. Usually quoted in Worldscale. This is the “window”.One print per Baltic day
Daily miniDFNA listed future on one index day. Lot = 100 t.Oct-05-26, Oct-06-26, … (~130 business days)
Monthly FFATDLListed future on the average of all Baltic days in the month. Lot = 1,000 t. This is the main paper people mean by “TD3C FFA”.Oct26, Nov26, …
BalmoTDMAverage of the days still left in the current month (already-printed days are out).Calendar dates
Mini means lot size, not a different index. DFN and TDL both settle off Baltic TD3C in $/t. Mini = 100 t vs 1,000 t, like mini Brent vs full-size.

There is no separate ICE “spot” feed in our Snowflake. Prompt DFN (the daily strip whose date equals the session) is the closest $/t proxy for that day’s print — it is still paper, not the Baltic tape.

DFN is a strip of one-day contracts. When Monday’s print is done and that contract’s last trading day has passed, Oct-05-26 is finished. Tuesday you trade Oct-06-26. It is not one future that rolls forever.

The usual mistake is “DFN = ICE Brent, TDL = the swap”. Flip it: TDL is the monthly you trade as “the FFA”; DFN is a daily window future. TDL happens to cash-settle on the average of prints, so it also plays the swap’s role.

4. Worked example — a four-day October

Toy month with four Baltic days. Everything in $/t. Market starts convinced every day will print 200.

Day 1 prints hot: Baltic = 240

DFN day 1 is paper on today’s window, so it trades into 240.

Case A — one-off spike. Days 2–4 still expected 200:

TDL = (240 + 200 + 200 + 200) / 4 = 210

You see print 240, DFN today 240, monthly only 210. That is the same shape as a live 5 Oct 2026 snapshot: DFN prompt ~251 $/t vs TDL Oct-26 ~238 $/t.

Case B — the spike sticks. Days 2–4 reprice to 230:

TDL = (240 + 230 + 230 + 230) / 4 = 232.5

Print and DFN today still 240. TDL moves more because the other daily futures (the rest of the window strip) repriced.

After day 2 prints 220 (days 3–4 still 200)

TDL = (240 + 220 + 200 + 200) / 4 = 215

InstrumentLevelStatus
DFN day 1240Done — that window is history
DFN day 2220Today’s window

TDM balmo200Only days 3–4: (200+200)/2. Does not keep the 240 and 220

Month end (days 3 and 4 print 200)

Month average = (240 + 220 + 200 + 200) / 4 = 215

TDL expires at 215. That is FFA settlement — the same idea as a Dated monthly swap fixing on the average of window prints.

Baltic = the print. DFN today = paper on that print. TDL = paper on all prints this month, averaged. They only sit on top of each other if every remaining day is expected to look like today.

Implied rest of month (real TDL, any day)

Once some Baltic days have printed, part of TDL is already known:

implied_rest = (TDL × N − sum of prints so far) / (N − n)

where N is index days in the month and n is days already published. Front-month TDL minus spot is mostly a view on those remaining days.

5. “Last trades on the second business day after the nominal date”

Each DFN strip has a nominal date: the Baltic index day it belongs to. ICE does not kill the contract at midnight on that date. Last trading day is the second business day after the nominal date. Final settlement is still that nominal day’s Baltic, in $/t.

Example: DFN Oct-05-26. 5 Oct 2026 was a Monday.

Mon 5 OctNominal date. Baltic publishes Monday’s TD3C. This is the print the contract is tied to.
Tue 6 Oct1st business day after. You can still trade Oct-05-26.
Wed 7 Oct2nd business day after = last trading day. After this close, the contract expires and cash-settles on Monday’s print — not Tuesday’s or Wednesday’s.

The extra sessions are a short window to trade around a print that is out or just coming out. They are not two extra index days.

Friday nominal: Fri 9 Oct → skip the weekend → 1st BD Mon 12 Oct, last trade Tue 13 Oct. The contract is still “Friday’s Baltic”.

December is special: ICE stops the daily series on the 24th (or previous business day) rather than running through year-end.

TDL is the opposite calendar: last trading day is the last trading day of the contract month (December TDL also uses the 24th rule). Settlement = average of Baltic prints over that month’s index days.

6. Units — Worldscale is not a dollar

Worldscale (WS) is a percentage of that route’s annual flat rate ($/t), reset 1 January.

usd_t = flat_rate × WS / 100

WS 100 in 2025 and WS 100 in 2026 are not the same dollars. For any history or curve that crosses 1 Jan, convert to $/t (or TCE) first. ICE tanker FFAs store and settle $/t, even when brokers still shout WS.

Rough sanity: a normal-year TD3C might sit WS 50–70 (~$1.5–2/bbl). Extreme 2026 prints (WS ~1,240) were a war-risk regime, not a “new normal” to calibrate models on.

Other quotes: some Baltic routes are lumpsum $ (e.g. TD22) or TCE $/day. Never mix those in one series. usd_bbl ≈ usd_t / 7.33 for crude.

TCE (time-charter equivalent) = (gross freight − bunkers − port costs) / round-voyage days. Owners think in TCE; FFAs on these routes settle in $/t.

7. How ICE lists the paper

  • Canonical monthly for TD3C is TDL. Daily mini DFN, balmo TDM. Average-price options exist on TDL.
  • Snowflake ICE.MFT.FUTURES has monthlies only as Oct26-style strips — no Q or Cal contracts. Quarters and calendars are packs of those months (usually equal-weighted average of the three or twelve TDL months).
  • Each session can appear twice: FILE_FLAG = Early (settle and volume, OI empty) then Final (OI filled). Use Final for positioning; Early is fine for the same-day curve. That is the ICE file dump, not a freight concept.
  • Product codes were recoded around Dec 2024 on some clean/dirty routes (e.g. TC14 WCN → WHK). TD3C monthly stayed TDL.

Other Baltic monthlies you will meet: TD20 TDC (name has no “FFA”), TD22 WF2, TC14 WHK, TC2 WNS. Same mechanics, different route. See docs/ice_ffa_map.md in this repo.

8. What “the market” is pricing

A steep TDL backwardation (front month far above the 12th month) means the screen thinks today’s crisis premium fades. A daily print far above TDL means either (a) today is a spike vs the rest of the month, or (b) the remaining days have not caught up yet — compute implied rest before telling a story.

Open interest lives on the monthly (TDL), not on the Baltic print. Falling OI with a still-steep curve is often paper getting out of a regime it does not want to own in the back, not “the physical list just got longer”.
